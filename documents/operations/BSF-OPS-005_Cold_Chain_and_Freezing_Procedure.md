## Purpose

This procedure defines how BlueSea Foods controls temperature during receiving, handling, chilling, freezing, frozen storage, loading, dispatch, and transport interface activities. It explains the operating rules that support food safety, quality, product identity, traceability, customer requirements, certification expectations, and HACCP-based control of seafood products.

The procedure complements BSF-OPS-001 - Cold Chain Control Register. The register stores temperature evidence; this procedure explains how the process works, why it matters, what records are required, and how deviations must be managed.

---

## Scope

This procedure applies to seafood raw material, chilled product, frozen product, semi-processed product, packed product, samples, returns, transfers, and export shipments handled by BlueSea Foods or by approved third parties working under BlueSea Foods instruction.

It covers:

- receiving temperature checks;
- chilled product handling;
- freezing operations;
- frozen product storage;
- cold-room and freezer monitoring;
- loading and dispatch;
- reefer container or refrigerated vehicle checks;
- BlueTrack temperature records;
- product hold and corrective action for deviations.

Product-specific temperature limits must be confirmed in the applicable specification, HACCP plan, customer requirement, legal requirement, or approved technical instruction. Where requirements differ, the stricter applicable requirement must be followed.

## Quick Answers for AI Retrieval

### What is the cold chain?

The cold chain is the temperature-controlled handling of seafood from receiving through processing, storage, dispatch, transport, and delivery. Its purpose is to preserve food safety, quality, freshness, product identity, and customer compliance by preventing uncontrolled warming, thawing, refreezing, or temperature abuse.

### Why is temperature control important in seafood?

Temperature control is important because seafood can lose quality quickly and may become unsafe if exposed to inappropriate temperatures. Poor control may support microbial growth, histamine formation in susceptible species, texture damage, thawing, dehydration, freezer burn, or noncompliance with customer and legal requirements.

### What are freezing requirements?

Freezing requirements are the approved time-temperature and handling conditions used to freeze seafood safely and consistently. BlueSea Foods uses approved freezing equipment, defined product loading practices, monitoring records, and Quality Assurance review to confirm that frozen products reach and maintain the required condition before storage or dispatch.

### What is Temperature Status?

Temperature Status is the system or record classification that indicates whether a temperature reading is acceptable, close to a limit, missing evidence, or outside the approved requirement. Typical status values are Within Limit, Warning, and Deviation.

### What happens if a temperature is outside the limit?

If a temperature is outside the approved limit, the product or location must be reviewed immediately. The responsible person records the reading, protects the product, notifies the supervisor and Quality Assurance, places affected product on hold when required, investigates the cause, and documents corrective action before release.

### What is the Cold Chain Module?

The Cold Chain Module is the BlueTrack area used to record product and storage temperature readings, monitor alerts, link evidence, create or connect corrective actions, and support review of cold chain deviations.

## Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Cold chain | Temperature-controlled handling from receiving to dispatch and delivery. |
| Chilled product | Product maintained above its freezing point within an approved low-temperature range. |
| Frozen product | Product maintained at or below its approved frozen storage temperature. |
| Cold storage | Temperature-controlled room, freezer, or warehouse used to preserve product condition. |
| Blast freezer | Equipment that rapidly lowers product temperature using controlled cold air movement. |
| Freezing tunnel | Continuous or semi-continuous equipment used to freeze product through controlled airflow and residence time. |
| Temperature reading | Measured temperature value recorded with date, time, location, product or equipment reference, unit, and responsible person. |
| Temperature limit | Approved boundary established by specification, procedure, HACCP plan, customer requirement, or law. |
| Warning | Temperature status showing that a reading is near a limit, lacks evidence, or requires prompt review. |
| Deviation | Failure to meet the approved temperature requirement or required monitoring condition. |
| Temperature excursion | Exposure outside an established temperature range. |
| Product hold | Controlled status preventing product use, release, movement, sale, or dispatch until an authorized decision is documented. |

## Cold Chain Principles

BlueSea Foods applies the following principles:

- Food safety and product integrity take priority over production speed or dispatch urgency.
- Temperature must be measured using approved or verified equipment.
- Product must not be released when required temperature evidence is missing or unacceptable.
- Temperature readings must be attributable to a product, lot, location, equipment, date, time, and responsible person.
- Product exposed to uncertain conditions must be held until Quality Assurance completes review.
- Chilled and frozen products must be protected from unnecessary exposure during unloading, staging, processing, loading, and dispatch.
- Temperature deviations must be documented, investigated, corrected, and reviewed for recurrence.

## Temperature Monitoring Points

| Process Stage | What to Check | Typical Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving | Product temperature, vehicle/container temperature, evidence of thawing, condition of ice or packaging. | Receiving record, thermometer reading, photo, transport log. |
| Chilled staging | Room temperature, product exposure time, door control, product condition. | Cold-room log, BlueTrack reading, supervisor check. |
| Processing | Product temperature where required, time outside controlled storage, line delays. | Process record, QA check, hold record if needed. |
| Freezing | Equipment status, loading pattern, start/end time, product condition, completion evidence. | Freezer record, batch record, BlueTrack entry. |
| Frozen storage | Freezer temperature, alarm status, door control, product segregation. | Cold storage log, alarm record, BlueTrack alert. |
| Loading | Product temperature, loading duration, vehicle or reefer condition, seal number. | Dispatch record, reefer printout, loading checklist. |
| Transport interface | Reefer set point, container pre-cooling where required, seal integrity. | Transport record, container checklist, photo evidence. |

## Temperature Status Rules

| Status | Meaning | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| Within Limit | Reading is within the approved requirement for the product, location, or equipment. | Continue monitoring and record evidence. |
| Warning | Reading is close to a limit, missing evidence, inconsistent with the product/location, or requires prompt review. | Review promptly, add comments or evidence, increase monitoring if required. |
| Deviation | Reading is outside the approved limit or required monitoring was missed. | Notify supervisor and QA, protect affected product, place on hold when required, investigate, document corrective action. |
| Unknown | Temperature history cannot be confirmed. | Treat as potential deviation, hold affected product, review exposure and evidence. |

## General Decision Logic

| Condition | Initial Decision | Escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Product temperature acceptable and records complete. | Continue process or release to next approved step. | Supervisor review as required. |
| Minor temperature concern with no evidence of product damage. | Hold or conditional acceptance pending QA review. | QA Manager. |
| Significant temperature deviation. | Product hold. | QA Manager and Food Safety Coordinator. |
| Visible thawing, decomposition, or unknown exposure time. | Product hold or rejection. | QA Manager and Food Safety Coordinator. |
| Freezer or cold-room failure. | Protect product, move product if needed, document affected scope. | Operations, QA, Maintenance. |
| Missing temperature records. | Hold affected product or record exception. | QA Manager. |
| Certified product affected by temperature event. | Maintain identity and restrict release until impact is assessed. | QA and Traceability Officer. |

## BlueTrack Recording Rules

Temperature records in BlueTrack must include:

- date and time;
- product, lot, location, or equipment ID;
- temperature value and unit;
- reading method or equipment used where required;
- responsible person;
- applicable status;
- comments for warnings or deviations;
- evidence attachment when required;
- link to product hold, CAPA, or nonconformity record when applicable.

BlueTrack should not be used to invent missing readings. If a reading was missed, the user must record the exception and escalate according to QA instructions.

---

## Corrective Action for Temperature Deviations

When a deviation occurs, the responsible employee shall:

1. Stop or control the activity when safe and practical.
2. Identify the affected product, lot, location, time window, equipment, and records.
3. Notify the supervisor and Quality Assurance.
4. Place affected product on hold when product safety, quality, legality, certification, or customer requirement may be affected.
5. Record the deviation in BlueTrack or the approved register.
6. Investigate the cause, such as door left open, equipment failure, wrong set point, delayed loading, incorrect product location, missing pre-cooling, or monitoring error.
7. Define immediate correction and corrective action.
8. Obtain QA disposition before release.
9. Verify effectiveness when required.
