## Purpose

This manual establishes the BlueSea Foods framework for preventing, eliminating, or reducing food safety hazards to acceptable levels throughout the sourcing, receiving, processing, packing, storage, and dispatch of seafood products. It defines the HACCP-based management system, prerequisite programmes, decision-making rules, evidence requirements, escalation duties, and governance controls that protect consumers, customers, certification status, and the BlueSea Foods brand.

The manual also provides a common corporate basis for site HACCP plans. It does not replace product- or process-specific plans. Each operating site shall maintain validated HACCP studies covering its products, process flow, equipment, packaging, intended use, customers, and legal market requirements.

---

## Scope

This manual applies to all BlueSea Foods employees, temporary workers, contractors, visitors, processing plants, cold stores, distribution operations, sourcing offices, and third parties whose activities may affect product safety. It covers raw materials, ingredients, processing aids, water and ice, packaging, rework, finished products, cleaning materials, storage conditions, transport interfaces, and product information.

The requirements apply to fresh, frozen, cooked, ready-to-eat, and value-added seafood products handled by BlueSea Foods. Site-specific HACCP plans may include additional controls for species, origin, allergens, parasites, histamine-forming fish, microbiological risks, or customer specifications. No local procedure may weaken this manual without written Corporate Food Safety approval.

## Definitions

**HACCP:** Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point system used to identify, evaluate, and control food safety hazards.

**Hazard:** A biological, chemical, physical, or allergen-related agent or condition with the potential to cause an adverse health effect.

**Critical Control Point (CCP):** A process step at which control is essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a significant food safety hazard to an acceptable level.

**Operational Prerequisite Programme (OPRP):** A control measure managed through operational criteria where loss of control could increase food safety risk, but which is not managed as a CCP.

**Critical Limit:** A measurable or observable boundary separating acceptability from unacceptability at a CCP.

**Corrective Action:** Action taken when monitoring shows that a critical limit or operational criterion has not been met, including control of affected product and removal of the cause.

**Hold:** Controlled status preventing use, release, or dispatch of product until an authorized disposition decision is documented.

## Main Content

### 1. Food Safety Policy and Core Principles

BlueSea Foods shall manufacture and supply seafood products that are safe, legal, authentic, and compliant with agreed specifications. Food safety takes precedence over production volume, dispatch deadlines, commercial pressure, or cost. No employee may release, use, rework, or dispatch product that is known or reasonably suspected to be unsafe.

The management system is based on the following principles: leadership accountability; hazard-based preventive control; hygienic design and operation; verified cold chain management; competent personnel; traceability; timely escalation; factual records; continual improvement; and transparent communication with customers and authorities when required.

Food safety objectives are established annually at corporate and site level. At minimum they shall address HACCP-plan review completion, CCP and OPRP compliance, verification completion, corrective-action closure, customer complaints, audit findings, training completion, and traceability exercise performance. Results are reviewed in management review meetings and used to set improvement actions.

### 2. HACCP Team and Plan Development

Every processing site shall appoint a multidisciplinary HACCP Team led by a competent HACCP Team Leader. Members shall represent Food Safety or Quality Assurance, Operations, Engineering or Maintenance, Sanitation, Procurement or Receiving, Warehouse or Logistics, and other disciplines relevant to the process. The team may obtain independent technical advice where specialized expertise is necessary.

The HACCP Team shall describe each product and intended use, identify vulnerable consumers where applicable, prepare and verify a process flow diagram during actual operations, and conduct a documented hazard analysis at every step. The analysis shall consider biological hazards such as pathogens and parasites; chemical hazards such as residues, allergens, cleaning chemicals, histamine, and environmental contaminants; and physical hazards such as metal, glass, hard plastic, and bone fragments.

For every reasonably foreseeable hazard, the team shall document severity, likelihood, existing control measures, and the rationale for determining whether the hazard is significant. The decision tree or equivalent documented rationale shall identify whether the control is managed through a prerequisite programme, OPRP, CCP, supplier control, specification, or another validated measure. Generic copied plans are not acceptable without site-specific validation.

HACCP plans shall be reviewed at least annually and before any significant change. Triggers include a new species or product, new supplier or origin, process or equipment change, formulation or packaging change, changed legal requirement, adverse trend, recall, significant complaint, audit finding, or evidence that a control is ineffective.

### 3. Prerequisite Programmes

Effective prerequisite programmes are the foundation of HACCP. Each site shall maintain documented, implemented, and verified controls for personal hygiene; protective clothing; visitor control; cleaning and sanitation; pest management; water, ice, and air quality; waste handling; maintenance; calibration; glass and brittle plastic; chemical control; allergen control; supplier approval; storage; transport; foreign-body prevention; and staff training.

Facilities and equipment must be designed, maintained, and cleaned to prevent contamination. Food-contact surfaces shall be suitable for their intended use and accessible for cleaning and inspection. Temporary repairs must not create contamination risks. Engineering shall notify Quality Assurance before work that may affect hygienic zoning, food-contact equipment, or a validated process control.

Personnel shall report illness, infected wounds, gastrointestinal symptoms, or any condition that could compromise product safety. Supervisors shall ensure immediate exclusion or reassignment where necessary. Handwashing, protective clothing, behavior in production areas, and restrictions on jewelry, food, drink, smoking, and personal items shall be monitored routinely.

Allergen controls shall identify allergens present in raw materials, rework, processing aids, and packaging claims. Segregation, scheduling, validated cleaning, label verification, and line-clearance controls shall be used as applicable. A label or packaging mismatch is a food safety incident and requires immediate hold and escalation.

### 4. Control of Seafood-Specific Hazards

Sites shall apply controls proportionate to the seafood species and product category. Receiving controls shall confirm approved supplier status, product identity, lot information, condition of transport, packaging integrity, sensory condition, temperature, and relevant certificates or declarations. Any material failing acceptance criteria shall be rejected or placed on hold pending documented Quality Assurance disposition.

For histamine-forming species, the HACCP plan shall define approved sources, time-temperature controls, sampling or supplier verification requirements, limits, and action rules. For products where parasite risk is relevant, the plan shall specify the validated treatment, time-temperature parameters, verification evidence, and product identification controls. For ready-to-eat products, environmental and microbiological monitoring shall be risk based and linked to sanitation, zoning, and product-release decisions.

Temperature control is critical across receiving, processing, freezing, storage, loading, and transport. Site limits and monitoring frequencies shall be defined in the applicable HACCP plan and BSF-OPS-001 — Cold Chain Control Register. A temperature reading without product, location, time, equipment identification, and responsible person is not an acceptable record.

### 5. CCP and OPRP Management

Each CCP shall have a documented hazard, control measure, critical limit, monitoring method, frequency, responsible role, record, immediate correction, corrective action, verification activity, and validation basis. Critical limits must be measurable or clearly observable and must not rely on subjective judgement alone where an objective method is available.

Monitoring must provide information in time to prevent release of affected product. The responsible operator records the result contemporaneously and immediately informs the supervisor or Quality Assurance of any deviation. Supervisors shall not alter a result or instruct personnel to continue operation outside a critical limit.

OPRPs shall be managed using defined operational criteria, monitoring, responsible roles, correction requirements, and verification. Examples may include label verification, sieve integrity, sanitation pre-operational inspection, chilled-product handling, or allergen changeover checks when identified by the site HACCP analysis. The HACCP plan, not the example, determines the actual classification.

Quality Assurance shall verify CCP and OPRP records at the frequency stated in the plan. Verification includes record review, direct observation, instrument checks, internal audit, testing, trend analysis, and review of corrective actions. Calibration status must be confirmed before instruments are used to make release or disposition decisions.

### 6. Deviations, Product Hold, and Disposition

When a CCP critical limit or OPRP criterion is not met, the responsible employee shall stop or control the affected activity when safe to do so, identify the deviation, notify the supervisor and Quality Assurance, and place all potentially affected material under hold. The hold scope shall be based on the last documented acceptable check, process conditions, lot traceability, and risk assessment.

Quality Assurance shall document the immediate correction, product evaluation, root-cause investigation, and final disposition. Disposition may include reprocessing under an approved validated method, downgrading where lawful and safe, destruction, return to supplier, or release only when objective evidence confirms safety and legality. Commercial urgency is never a basis for release.

If affected product may have left BlueSea Foods control, the Corporate Food Safety Manager shall assess the need for withdrawal, recall, customer notification, regulatory notification, or intensified traceability. The process shall follow BSF-OPS-005 — Product Traceability, Withdrawal and Recall Procedure. All deviations shall be evaluated for trends and recurring causes under BSF-QMS-003 — Corrective Action and Nonconformity Procedure.

### 7. Product Release, Traceability, and Food Defense Interface

Finished product may be released only by authorized personnel after confirming that applicable CCP and OPRP records are complete and acceptable, product specifications are met, required testing or certificates are available, labeling is verified, and unresolved food safety holds do not exist. Release authorization shall be traceable to the lot or production batch.

The traceability system must connect incoming material, processing or packing records, rework where used, packaging, finished lots, storage location, and dispatch information. Sites shall test traceability at planned intervals and demonstrate rapid identification of affected products. Reconciliation failures, untraceable rework, or missing lot records require immediate escalation.

Food safety controls shall be coordinated with security and food defense controls. Unauthorized access, suspected tampering, unusual packages, compromised seals, or loss of custody must be reported immediately to site management, Quality Assurance, and Security in accordance with applicable BASC controls.

### 8. Validation, Verification, and Continuous Improvement

Validation demonstrates, before use or following change, that a control measure is capable of achieving the intended food safety outcome. Validation may use legal requirements, authoritative guidance, scientific data, challenge studies, supplier evidence, equipment data, or documented expert assessment. Validation evidence shall be available to auditors and reviewers.

Verification confirms that the validated system is being followed and remains effective. The site verification programme shall include scheduled record review, pre-operational inspections, internal audits, calibration checks, microbiological or chemical testing where applicable, environmental monitoring, complaint review, and trend analysis. Failures shall result in documented correction and CAPA.

The Corporate Food Safety Manager shall review food safety performance at least quarterly. Significant risks, repeated deviations, product holds, recall simulations, customer complaints, emerging hazards, and certification findings shall be escalated to Corporate Management with action owners and due dates.

### 9. RAG and AI-Assisted Use of Food Safety Content

The controlled repository may support authorized employees in locating approved requirements, responsible roles, forms, and escalation pathways. AI-assisted responses must cite or link to the applicable controlled document and must not replace food safety judgment, product release authorization, risk assessment, or regulatory reporting decisions.

An AI assistant must not determine that product is safe, authorize a deviation, set a new critical limit, or infer missing monitoring evidence. If source material is incomplete, contradictory, expired, or outside the user’s authorization, the user shall be directed to Quality Assurance or the Corporate Food Safety Manager. Only current approved HACCP documents may be indexed for production retrieval.

## Roles and Responsibilities

Corporate Management provides resources, reviews food safety performance, and ensures that food safety is not compromised by commercial priorities.

The Corporate Food Safety Manager owns this manual, approves corporate HACCP governance, supports site escalation, and reports material risks to Corporate Management.

The Site HACCP Team Leader maintains the site HACCP plan, coordinates reviews and validation, ensures team competence, and initiates change review.

The Quality Assurance Manager verifies implementation, controls holds and product disposition, reviews records, manages investigations, and authorizes product release within delegated authority.

Operations Managers ensure that approved controls are followed, personnel are available and competent, deviations are promptly reported, and production does not continue under uncontrolled conditions.

Supervisors perform or confirm monitoring, ensure complete records, apply immediate controls, and escalate deviations without delay.

Employees and contractors follow hygiene and process controls, make accurate records, stop and report unsafe conditions, and never bypass a food safety control.

Engineering and Maintenance maintain equipment, protect hygienic conditions during work, and obtain Quality Assurance review for changes affecting food safety.

Procurement approves only qualified suppliers and communicates supplier or material changes to Food Safety and Quality Assurance.
