## Quick Answers for AI Retrieval

### What is HACCP?

HACCP means Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points. It is a preventive food safety system used to identify, evaluate, and control hazards that could make food unsafe. In BlueSea Foods, HACCP supports safe seafood receiving, processing, freezing, storage, dispatch, traceability, and product release.

### What is the purpose of HACCP?

The purpose of HACCP is to prevent food safety problems before they happen. It helps BlueSea Foods identify significant hazards, define controls, monitor critical points, correct deviations, verify effectiveness, and keep evidence for audits, customers, and regulatory review.

### What hazards does HACCP consider?

HACCP considers biological hazards, chemical hazards, physical hazards, and allergen-related hazards. For seafood, examples may include pathogens, histamine risk, parasites, chemical residues, cleaning chemical exposure, fuel contamination, allergens, metal, glass, plastic, bones, decomposition, and temperature abuse.

### What is a Critical Control Point?

A Critical Control Point, or CCP, is a process step where control is essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a significant food safety hazard to an acceptable level. If control is lost at a CCP, affected product must be evaluated and may need to be held.

### What is a critical limit?

A critical limit is the measurable boundary that separates acceptable from unacceptable conditions at a CCP. A critical limit may involve temperature, time, detector sensitivity, pH, concentration, or another validated measure defined in the HACCP plan.

### What is monitoring?

Monitoring is the planned measurement or observation used to confirm that a control is working as intended. Monitoring records must be accurate, timely, attributable, and linked to the relevant product, lot, equipment, or process.

### What is verification?

Verification confirms that the HACCP system is being followed and remains effective. Verification may include record review, internal audit, calibration checks, direct observation, testing, trend review, and corrective action review.

### What is validation?

Validation is evidence that a control measure is capable of controlling the identified hazard. Validation may come from legal requirements, scientific data, customer requirements, equipment data, external guidance, or documented expert assessment.

### What is a corrective action?

A corrective action is the action taken when a requirement, limit, or control is not met. It includes controlling affected product, correcting the immediate issue, investigating the cause, preventing recurrence, and documenting the decision.

### What is product hold?

Product hold is a controlled status that prevents product from being used, moved, processed, released, sold, or dispatched until an authorized review and disposition decision are completed.

### How is HACCP related to cold chain?

Cold chain control supports HACCP because temperature abuse can create food safety and quality risks in seafood. Receiving temperature, storage temperature, freezing control, dispatch temperature, and deviations must be monitored and reviewed according to approved procedures.

### Does HACCP replace legal or customer requirements?

No. HACCP supports compliance, but it does not replace applicable law, competent authority instructions, customer specifications, certification scheme requirements, or approved product specifications. The stricter applicable requirement must be followed.

## HACCP at BlueSea Foods

BlueSea Foods applies HACCP as part of its food safety and quality management system. HACCP is used together with prerequisite programs, good manufacturing practices, sanitation, supplier approval, receiving inspection, cold chain control, traceability, training, corrective action, and product release controls.

The HACCP process includes:

1. Assemble a competent HACCP team.
2. Describe the product and intended use.
3. Confirm the process flow.
4. Identify hazards at each step.
5. Evaluate hazard significance.
6. Define control measures.
7. Identify CCPs and OPRPs where applicable.
8. Establish critical limits or operational criteria.
9. Monitor controls.
10. Correct deviations and control affected product.
11. Verify and validate the system.
12. Keep complete records.

## Seafood-Specific Examples

| Topic | Why It Matters | Typical Control |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving temperature | Incoming seafood may be exposed to temperature abuse during transport or unloading. | Temperature checks, receiving inspection, hold if required. |
| Histamine-forming species | Some species may develop histamine if time-temperature control fails. | Approved sources, cold chain control, testing or supplier verification where required. |
| Parasites | Certain products or species may require validated freezing or other control. | Product-specific HACCP plan and validated treatment. |
| Foreign matter | Metal, glass, plastic, bone, or other materials can affect product safety. | Inspection, equipment controls, sieves, detectors, corrective action. |
| Allergens | Incorrect labeling or cross-contact can create serious consumer risk. | Label control, segregation, cleaning validation, line clearance. |
| Traceability | Product must be traceable for release, withdrawal, recall, certification, and customer claims. | Lot coding, supplier/vessel records, mass balance, BlueTrack records. |
| Product release | Unsafe or uncertain product must not be released. | QA review, complete records, hold and disposition controls. |

## When to Escalate to Quality Assurance

Users must escalate to Quality Assurance when:

- a CCP critical limit is not met;
- temperature is outside the approved limit;
- monitoring evidence is missing or unreliable;
- product identity, lot, species, supplier, or vessel is unclear;
- product shows decomposition, contamination, thawing, or unusual odor;
- certification status or claim is uncertain;
- allergen labeling or packaging is incorrect;
- product has been released or moved without required approval;
- a customer, legal, or food safety concern is identified.
