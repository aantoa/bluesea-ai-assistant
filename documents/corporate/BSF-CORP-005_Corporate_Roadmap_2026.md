It simulates an internal strategic roadmap for a multinational seafood and food processing company.

This roadmap does not replace approved annual budgets, customer commitments, certification requirements, legal obligations, site operating plans, or decisions reserved to Corporate Management. It sets the shared direction for 2026. Where a legal, food safety, customer, or certification requirement is more specific or more stringent, that requirement shall prevail. ## Purpose

The purpose of the Corporate Roadmap 2026 is to translate BlueSea Foods' corporate direction into priorities, outcomes, governance practices, and measurable initiatives for the period from July through December 2026.

The roadmap aligns corporate functions, processing facilities, cold-storage operations, sourcing offices, commercial teams, and support services around a common operating agenda. It is designed to strengthen profitable and responsible growth; protect food safety, quality, and traceability; improve operational reliability; build a capable workforce; enhance digital and data governance; and maintain the trust of customers, suppliers, communities, and regulators.

It also provides a controlled strategic source for the Corporate Knowledge Repository. Employees and authorized AI assistants may use it to understand priorities, locate accountable functions, and identify when a matter must be escalated.

---

## Scope

This roadmap applies to all BlueSea Foods business units, corporate functions, processing plants, cold-storage facilities, distribution activities, sourcing operations, and authorized support providers. It applies to strategic initiatives, cross-functional programs, departmental objectives, and major improvement projects during 2026.

The roadmap covers enterprise priorities rather than detailed work instructions. Site-level production schedules, capital authorization documents, sales forecasts, supplier agreements, quality plans, HR plans, and technology specifications remain governed by their respective approved documents. This roadmap does not authorize expenditure, a change to a food safety control, a certification claim, a data-processing activity, or a contractual commitment without the approvals required by the applicable policy or procedure.

## Definitions

**Strategic Initiative:** A time-bound, approved program or project that materially supports one or more corporate priorities and has a designated sponsor, owner, plan, resources, measures, and reporting cadence.

**Enterprise KPI:** A key performance indicator used by Corporate Management to evaluate progress or effectiveness across more than one department, site, or business unit.

**Initiative Sponsor:** A member of senior management who removes barriers, confirms business alignment, and escalates decisions beyond the authority of the initiative owner.

**Milestone:** A verifiable planned result that indicates completion of a defined stage, deliverable, approval, or readiness condition.

**Corrective Action:** Action taken to eliminate the cause of a detected nonconformity or performance failure and prevent recurrence.

## Main Content

### 1. Strategic Direction

BlueSea Foods will pursue responsible growth in seafood and food markets by combining customer focus, operational discipline, certification integrity, and sustainable value creation. The Company shall not pursue commercial growth at the expense of food safety, legality, worker welfare, product traceability, cargo security, ethical conduct, or data protection.

During 2026, the Company will make management systems easier to execute and verify. Requirements must be visible in daily work, records must be reliable, performance issues must be addressed at source, and decisions must be based on current authorized information. Departments shall connect local objectives to these priorities and avoid isolated projects without a clear business, risk, or customer benefit.

### 2. Corporate Priorities for 2026

The following priorities are mandatory planning themes. Their relative importance may differ by function, but none may be disregarded by a site or department.

| Priority | Intended outcome by the end of 2026 | Corporate expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Safe, legal, and trusted products | Food safety, quality, traceability, and certification controls are consistently executed and evidenced. | Prevent nonconformities; act immediately on deviations; protect product integrity. |
| Customer-led profitable growth | Commercial growth is supported by reliable service, disciplined pricing, and capability to meet approved market requirements. | Prioritize sustainable margin, service, and long-term customer trust. |
| Operational excellence and resilience | Sites reduce avoidable loss, improve cold-chain and production reliability, and strengthen continuity readiness. | Use data to address recurring causes, not only visible symptoms. |
| Responsible sourcing and sustainability | Supply-chain decisions protect legality, responsible sourcing commitments, and transparent product claims. | Maintain verified traceability and responsible supplier relationships. |
| People, leadership, and culture | Employees understand their responsibilities, develop competence, and can speak up without retaliation. | Build accountability, respectful collaboration, and safe work practices. |
| Digital, data, and knowledge enablement | Approved digital tools improve access to controlled knowledge and meaningful performance insight. | Secure information, preserve human accountability, and avoid uncontrolled AI use. |

### 3. Safe, Legal, and Trusted Products

Food safety and product integrity remain non-negotiable. Each site shall maintain effective prerequisite programs, HACCP controls, supplier and material controls, traceability, allergen management where applicable, sanitation verification, product-release discipline, and timely management of complaints and incidents. The Food Safety Manager and Quality Assurance Manager shall ensure that significant risks, trends, and unresolved corrective actions are visible to site and corporate leadership.

In 2026, priority effort shall be placed on verifying that procedures reflect actual practice and that records demonstrate execution. A complete record is not merely a signed form: it must be legible, contemporaneous, attributable, accurate, and retrievable. Repeated deviations, incomplete records, or delayed verification shall be treated as management-system risks, even when no unsafe product has been released.

Sites shall maintain certification readiness for applicable BRCGS Food Safety, IFS Food, MSC Chain of Custody, BASC, customer, and regulatory requirements. Certification activities must be integrated into routine operations rather than managed as an isolated pre-audit exercise. Product claims, including MSC claims, may only be used after the required verification and authorization.

### 4. Customer-Led Profitable Growth

Commercial teams shall develop growth opportunities through a clear understanding of customer requirements, product capability, service reliability, and approved profitability criteria. New business, product, market, or customer commitments must be evaluated with the operational functions that will deliver them. A commercial promise must not be made before relevant requirements for specification, capacity, food safety, traceability, labeling, logistics, payment, and regulatory conditions have been assessed.

Sales volume alone is not a sufficient success measure. Commercial decisions shall consider contribution margin, payment risk, service performance, complaint trends, supply continuity, and required customization. Compliance shall be involved whenever a transaction presents integrity, sanctions, government-interaction, or third-party risk.

### 5. Operational Excellence and Resilience

Operations shall improve reliability from raw-material reception through production, storage, dispatch, and delivery. The focus is on reducing preventable disruption, loss, rework, energy waste, temperature excursions, delayed release, and avoidable customer service failures. Operations Managers shall use daily and weekly performance routines to identify deviations, assign ownership, verify containment, and ensure that recurring issues receive root-cause analysis.

Each site shall identify critical dependencies, including utilities, refrigeration, key equipment, transport routes, packaging, labor availability, and digital systems. When an event threatens food safety, customer delivery, employee safety, cargo security, or regulatory compliance, personnel must use the established escalation process rather than wait for normal reporting cycles.

Improvement projects shall have a baseline, a measurable target, a responsible owner, an implementation plan, and a method to confirm that benefits are sustained. A project may not be closed solely because activities were completed; it must show that the agreed result was achieved or that a revised decision was approved.

### 6. Responsible Sourcing and Sustainability

BlueSea Foods shall manage responsible sourcing as a business requirement, not as a communications activity. Procurement, Quality, Sustainability, and Operations shall work together to ensure that approved suppliers meet applicable legal, food safety, social, ethical, and traceability expectations. Supplier performance shall be reviewed according to risk, product category, history, certification status, and business criticality.

The Company will improve visibility of origin, custody, and product claims across its supply chain. Personnel must preserve evidence supporting species, source, lot identity, supplier approval, and certification status. Where an MSC Chain of Custody control applies, the chain of custody must be maintained without substitution, commingling, mislabeling, or unsupported claim. Concerns about illegal, unethical, or non-compliant sourcing must be escalated immediately.

Environmental initiatives shall focus on practical improvements in energy, water, waste, packaging, cold-chain performance, and loss prevention. They may not weaken a mandatory food safety, quality, or customer control.

### 7. People, Leadership, and Culture

BlueSea Foods expects leaders to make responsibilities clear, provide resources and training, recognize safe and ethical conduct, and address performance issues fairly. Managers must not create an environment in which employees feel pressured to hide deviations, bypass controls, or avoid raising concerns. Speaking up in good faith shall be protected under BSF-COMP-001 — Corporate Compliance and Ethics Policy.

Departments shall maintain role-based competency plans for positions that influence food safety, quality, security, compliance, finance, people management, and critical operations. Training completion alone does not prove competence; supervisors must observe performance, coach, and arrange retraining when needed.

Cross-functional cooperation is essential. A problem affecting customer service, food safety, cost, or compliance rarely belongs to one function alone. Initiative owners must involve affected departments early, communicate decisions through approved channels, and document agreed actions and owners.

### 8. Digital, Data, and Knowledge Enablement

Digitalization in 2026 will focus on secure, useful, and controlled capabilities rather than technology adoption for its own sake. The Company will improve the accessibility of approved procedures, records, dashboards, and training content while preserving document control, confidentiality, privacy, access restrictions, and human review.

The Corporate Knowledge Repository and approved RAG tools may help employees locate current policies, understand procedures, and identify responsible roles. They do not replace authorization, professional judgment, or escalation. AI-generated responses must be checked against controlled sources for high-risk matters.

All digital initiatives shall be assessed for business purpose, process ownership, data classification, information security, privacy, integration risk, user training, support, continuity, and measurable value. Public AI tools and unapproved applications shall not receive confidential, restricted, personal, customer, supplier, security, or controlled-document information.

### 9. Initiative Planning and Delivery Requirements

Every strategic initiative shall have a documented charter or equivalent approved plan before execution begins. At minimum, the plan shall state the linked corporate priority, sponsor, owner, scope, expected benefits, risks, resources, milestones, measures, dependencies, and communication approach.

The initiative owner shall maintain an action log and provide status updates at the agreed cadence. Status reporting must distinguish completed milestones from pending actions and confirmed benefits from estimates. A status of “green” must not be used when a critical decision, resource, compliance review, or dependency remains unresolved.

Changes to scope, expected benefit, timing, budget, or control requirements must be assessed by the sponsor and relevant functional owners. Changes with food safety, legal, data-protection, certification, security, or significant financial impact require formal review before implementation.

### 10. Performance Monitoring and Escalation

Corporate Management shall review enterprise progress monthly, supported by a quarterly strategic review. Where applicable, measures may include food safety and audit performance, customer service, complaint trends, yield and waste, cold-chain reliability, supplier performance, employee competency, incident response, certification readiness, delivery reliability, and initiative progress.

Each KPI must have a definition, source, owner, reporting frequency, target or expected direction, and action rule when performance is outside tolerance. Manipulating a measure, excluding unfavorable data, or changing a definition without approval is prohibited.

The following conditions require prompt escalation to the relevant executive sponsor and, where applicable, Corporate Management: a food safety or legality concern; suspected product fraud; a major customer or regulatory risk; a serious security or privacy incident; a material ethical concern; a certification-threatening nonconformity; a major project delay; or an initiative that is likely to exceed approved authority or resources.

## Roles and Responsibilities

Corporate Management approves the roadmap, reviews enterprise progress, resolves priority conflicts, and authorizes material changes to strategic direction.

The Corporate Strategy Director owns the roadmap, coordinates reviews, consolidates performance information, and challenges unclear, unmeasurable, or misaligned initiatives.

Executive Sponsors ensure that assigned initiatives have strategic alignment, authority, resources, and timely decisions.

Initiative Owners develop and execute approved plans, manage risks and dependencies, maintain records, communicate progress, and escalate issues beyond their authority.

Site Directors and Department Heads translate relevant priorities into controlled operational plans, assign accountable personnel, and verify implementation within their areas.

The Food Safety Manager, Quality Assurance Manager, Compliance Officer, Information Security Manager, HR Manager, Finance Director, Sustainability Lead, and other subject matter experts review initiatives within their specialist authority and prevent changes that would weaken mandatory controls.

Employees support assigned actions, follow approved procedures, provide accurate information, and promptly raise risks, deviations, or improvement opportunities.
