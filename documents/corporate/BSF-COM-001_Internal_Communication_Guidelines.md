## Purpose

These guidelines establish how BlueSea Foods communicates internally so that employees receive timely, accurate, respectful, secure, and actionable information. They define approved communication channels, minimum standards for messages and meetings, responsibilities for corporate and site announcements, escalation rules, recordkeeping expectations, and safeguards for confidential information.

Consistent communication supports safe production, reliable cold-chain operations, employee engagement, coordinated incident response, audit readiness, and informed decision-making. The guidelines also ensure that internal AI assistants retrieve approved communication rules rather than informal practices or outdated messages.

---

## Scope

This document applies to directors, managers, supervisors, employees, contractors, temporary workers, and authorized consultants across BlueSea Foods corporate offices, plants, cold stores, sourcing locations, logistics operations, and commercial teams.

It covers internal email, Microsoft Teams or other approved collaboration platforms, meetings, internal announcements, noticeboards, intranet content, training communications, shift briefings, emergency alerts, and communication records. It applies whether communication is issued globally, regionally, by site, or within a department.

This document does not authorize employees to make public statements, respond to media, disclose customer information, publish product claims, or communicate with regulators on behalf of BlueSea Foods. Such communications are governed by the applicable approval process and must be referred to Corporate Communications, Compliance, Legal, or the designated site representative.

## Definitions

**Internal Communication:** Information shared within BlueSea Foods to support operations, employee understanding, coordination, compliance, or management decisions.

**Official Announcement:** A message approved by an authorized owner and distributed through an approved channel to communicate a policy, decision, operational requirement, or material business update.

**Sensitive Information:** Information that is confidential, personal, commercially restricted, legally protected, security-sensitive, or not approved for broad distribution.

**Escalation:** Prompt communication of an issue to the appropriate authority because it requires urgent action, specialist review, or management decision.

**Shift Briefing:** A structured communication delivered to operational personnel at the start or during a shift concerning safety, food safety, quality, production, security, or other applicable matters.

## Main Content

### 1. Communication Principles

All internal communication shall be accurate, clear, respectful, timely, and appropriate to the audience. Messages must state what has happened or is required, who is responsible, when action is due, which location or process is affected, and where recipients may obtain clarification. Managers shall avoid vague instructions such as “handle urgently” when they can identify the required action, deadline, and escalation contact.

Communication must reflect BlueSea Foods values and must never include discriminatory, abusive, threatening, misleading, retaliatory, or offensive language. Employees shall raise disagreements professionally and challenge decisions through the appropriate management, HR, Quality, Compliance, or whistleblowing channels.

Only verified information may be communicated as fact. Where an investigation or assessment is ongoing, the message shall identify the known facts, the owner of the review, any immediate control measures, and the next planned update. Employees must not speculate about incidents, disciplinary matters, customer complaints, food safety status, or commercial negotiations.

### 2. Selecting the Appropriate Channel

The channel must match the urgency, audience, sensitivity, and need for a record. Email is appropriate for formal instructions, approvals, project coordination, and information requiring a searchable record. Teams is appropriate for short operational coordination, collaboration, and scheduled calls, but it must not be used as the only channel for controlled policy changes or critical emergency notifications.

The intranet or controlled repository is the authoritative location for policies, procedures, forms, organizational notices, and approved guidance. A message announcing a revised document must link to or identify the controlled source; it must not attach an uncontrolled version as the sole reference.

Shift briefings and noticeboards are essential where personnel do not have routine computer access. Supervisors must communicate key instructions in a language and format understood by the workforce, confirm understanding through questions or attendance records where needed, and retain evidence for food safety, safety, quality, or compliance-critical topics.

Urgent alerts affecting product safety, employee safety, security, systems availability, or business continuity must be issued through the designated emergency notification channels and followed by direct escalation to the responsible manager. An email alone is not sufficient when immediate action is necessary.

### 3. Email and Collaboration Platform Rules

Employees shall use company accounts and approved platforms for business communication. Personal email accounts, personal messaging applications, and unapproved file-sharing services shall not be used to transmit confidential information, employee data, customer data, supplier pricing, audit evidence, security plans, or controlled documents.

Email subject lines must accurately describe the topic and, where relevant, identify urgency or required action. Use “Action Required,” “Decision Needed,” or “Information Only” only when those labels are accurate. Recipients must be selected carefully: use “To” for responsible action owners, “CC” for informed stakeholders, and distribution lists only when the message is relevant to all members.

Messages containing sensitive information shall be limited to authorized recipients and protected according to BSF-IT-003 — Data Security and API Guidelines. Before sending, employees must check attachments, links, recipient addresses, and document versions. Passwords, access credentials, confidential investigation materials, and special-category personal data shall never be sent through ordinary email unless an approved secure method has been authorized.

Teams channels shall be organized by business purpose and owned by a named manager. Conversations that result in an operational decision, approval, deviation, or corrective action must be recorded in the applicable controlled system, meeting minutes, change record, or action log. Informal chat history is not a substitute for required quality, food safety, HR, compliance, or audit records.

### 4. Meetings and Shift Briefings

Meeting organizers shall define the objective, participants, agenda, and expected decisions before scheduling a meeting. Meetings should include only individuals who contribute to the decision, provide required expertise, or need the information to perform their role. When a decision affects another function, that function must be represented or consulted before implementation.

Minutes are required for management reviews, food safety meetings, quality meetings, audits, CAPA reviews, incident reviews, supplier meetings involving commitments, and other meetings designated by a Process Owner. Minutes shall record the date, participants, matters discussed, decisions, action owner, deadline, and escalation requirements. The organizer shall issue minutes promptly and store them in the designated controlled location.

Shift briefings shall cover current risks and relevant updates, including changes to production instructions, allergen controls, cold-chain limits, hygiene requirements, security restrictions, corrective actions, and incidents. Supervisors must not assume that a message sent to a shared mailbox has reached shift personnel. Where understanding is critical, supervisors shall obtain questions, practical confirmation, sign-off, or training evidence.

### 5. Official Announcements and Document Changes

Official announcements may be issued only by Corporate Management, Corporate Communications, Human Resources, Quality Assurance, Food Safety, Compliance, Information Security, or another function authorized for the subject matter. Site managers may issue local operational notices within their authority, provided the notices do not conflict with corporate policy or controlled documents.

An announcement concerning a new or revised policy, procedure, system, organization change, or compliance obligation must include: the subject, effective date, affected audience, reason for change where appropriate, required action, owner for questions, training requirement if any, and a link or reference to the controlled document. If the change affects food safety, legality, certification, security, employee rights, or data privacy, the issuing function must confirm that relevant stakeholders have reviewed it.

Employees shall not alter corporate templates, logos, approved policy language, or document control details when redistributing an official announcement. Where local adaptation is necessary, it must be reviewed by the Process Owner and clearly identify the site applicability.

### 6. Escalation and Incident Communication

Any employee who becomes aware of a potential food safety, product legality, serious quality, employee safety, security, data privacy, ethical, or business continuity issue must immediately inform their supervisor and use the applicable escalation process. The employee does not need to complete an investigation before escalating; prompt reporting enables timely containment and assessment.

The following communication priorities apply:

| Event | Immediate notification | Required communication control |
|---|---|---|
| Food safety or product legality concern | Food Safety Manager and Quality Assurance Manager | Contain affected product; use the approved incident process and preserve records. |
| Cold-chain failure or critical quality deviation | Operations Manager and Quality Assurance Manager | Stop or segregate product where required; record time, temperature, product, and actions. |
| Data breach or suspected unauthorized access | Information Security Manager and Compliance Officer | Do not disclose further information; preserve evidence and follow the privacy incident process. |
| Security threat or suspicious cargo activity | Site Security Lead and BASC Coordinator | Use site emergency and BASC reporting channels; do not circulate sensitive route or cargo details. |
| Ethics concern, harassment, or retaliation allegation | Compliance Officer or HR Manager | Maintain confidentiality; use the approved reporting and investigation process. |

For material incidents, the designated incident leader shall issue controlled updates at a frequency proportionate to the risk. Updates must distinguish confirmed facts from pending findings and identify temporary instructions. Corporate Communications shall coordinate employee-wide messages where the event has broad operational, reputational, or employee impact.

### 7. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Respectful Conduct

Internal information must be shared only with people who have a legitimate business need. Employees must not post photographs of production areas, customer documents, shipments, employee records, audit findings, or internal screens on public platforms or unapproved private groups. Information received through an investigation, complaint, grievance, medical process, or ethics report must be handled on a strict need-to-know basis.

Personal data must be minimized in routine communications. For example, a manager coordinating a return-to-work arrangement should communicate operational coverage needs without sharing unnecessary medical details. Where employee data is required for a lawful business purpose, the sender must use the approved system and recipient group.

Employees must not use internal communication channels to spread rumors, campaign for personal commercial activity, distribute political material, or bypass established reporting channels. Concerns about bullying, discrimination, harassment, conflicts of interest, bribery, or retaliation shall be reported under BSF-COMP-001 — Corporate Compliance and Ethics Policy and the applicable HR process.

### 8. Communication Quality and Continuous Improvement

Managers shall periodically evaluate whether important communications reached the intended audience and led to the expected action. Evidence may include acknowledgement reports, attendance lists, training records, audit findings, completed action logs, employee feedback, or operational performance data.

Communication failures, including contradictory instructions, missing translations, late escalation, unauthorized disclosure, or inaccessible messages for shift workers, shall be corrected promptly. Repeated failures must be analyzed as a process issue and may require a corrective action, additional training, revision of a template, or improved communication channel.

Corporate Communications and Human Resources shall maintain practical templates for announcements, meeting minutes, change notices, and escalation updates. Templates support consistency but do not replace the need to tailor messages to the relevant risk and audience.

## Roles and Responsibilities

Corporate Management sets the tone for transparent, respectful, and accountable communication and approves corporate-wide communications of strategic significance.

The Corporate Communications Manager owns these guidelines, manages approved communication templates, coordinates major internal announcements, and supports incident communications with the designated business owner.

Department and Site Managers ensure that communications within their teams are accurate, timely, accessible, and aligned with controlled documents. They confirm that shift-based personnel receive critical updates.

Supervisors deliver shift briefings, verify understanding where required, record attendance or acknowledgement, and escalate concerns without delay.

The Human Resources Manager reviews communications affecting employee policies, labor relations, employee wellbeing, conduct, or organizational changes.

The Compliance Officer reviews ethics, investigations, regulatory, whistleblowing, and conflict-of-interest communications.

The Food Safety Manager, Quality Assurance Manager, Information Security Manager, and BASC Coordinator validate communications in their respective risk areas before broad release when required.

Employees and contractors use approved channels responsibly, protect confidential information, follow issued instructions, and report unclear, incorrect, or urgent matters promptly.

## Records

The following records shall be retained when applicable:

- Approved corporate and site announcements
- Meeting agendas, minutes, and action logs
- Shift briefing attendance or acknowledgement records
- Communication plans for major changes or incidents
- Employee feedback and communication issue logs
- Training records for communication-critical policies
- Incident updates and escalation records
- Evidence of document-change communication

Records shall be retained in accordance with BSF-DOC-001 — Document Control Procedure and applicable legal, certification, and contractual requirements.
